# Voice

**everybody Hz** is written lowercase "everybody", capital-H "Hz" (the SI unit). The name carries the joke, so the copy doesn't need to.

We sound like a good control room: calm, exact, on your side. The personality comes from precision and restraint, not from cheerfulness.

## Principles

1. **Facts first, then what to do.** Name the notice, give the time window, say what it means for a household.
2. **Specific beats soothing.** Say "NESO has asked generators for more headroom 16:00–19:00", not "things are a bit tight". Real numbers (49.97 Hz, 5.8 GW), real times and real sources.
3. **Say only what we know.** Don't promise reminders that aren't built, or say "enough capacity was found" when all we know is that a notice was cancelled.
4. **Tone follows the level.**

   | Level | Register | Example |
   |---|---|---|
   | All clear | Brief, dry | "Nothing to report." |
   | Grid notice | Matter-of-fact, explanatory | "A routine grid notice." |
   | Heads-up | Direct, measured | "Small chance of controlled cuts." |
   | Get ready | Instructions, short sentences | "Charge phones and power banks now." |
   | Power off | Minimum words, second person | "Block C is off until 19:30." |

5. **Dry wit is allowed only where nothing is at stake.** A line like "Every generator on the grid turns in step, 50 times a second" belongs in explanations and on calm screens. Never put a joke near a warning.

## Never

- Exclamation marks, emoji, or "Oops!"
- Faux-friendly filler: "we've got your back", "don't worry", "folks", "super", "simply", "just".
- Treating the grid as a character ("the grid's feeling tired").
- Puns in the copy. The name has used up the allowance.
- Fear: "blackout", "chaos" or "brace yourself". Say "power cut", "controlled cut" or "rotating power cut", the terms the industry and the 105 line use.
- Overclaiming: "prediction", "guarantee" or "we'll always warn you".

## House style

- British spelling, 24-hour clock (16:00–19:00, with an en dash), and "today" or "tomorrow" in place of dates where that's clearer.
- Units: Hz to two decimals, MW below 1,000, GW with one decimal above.
- Call NESO "NESO" (the National Energy System Operator) and give notices their official names on first mention in detail views.
- Give 105 as the number to call in a power cut, free in England, Scotland and Wales.
